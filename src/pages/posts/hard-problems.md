How will there be any senior software engineers in the future if there are no juniors today? This has been one of the hotly debated questions in recent times ever since the tech hiring has slowed down and chants of AI replacing software engineers began [insert some charts here]. Of course, the answer depends on which camp you're in. If you think AI will not replace software engineering any time soon, say 10-15 years, then it's a legitimate case for worry. But if you think at least most of what software engineering is today will change if not completely replaced by AI in the next few years then.

Like most people, I'm not sure of the timelines but I'd like to consider this point from a different angle. What does most of software engineering comprise of?

Outline:
- Explore how SWE is a major economic expense with talent shortage hence the goal of automating it. Need to cite reliable sources here.
- The question: will AI replace swe? One of the biggest arguments is that AI still struggles with context and system design esp in large codebases. 
- Offer a different perspective: what will software look like in the next few years or decade for most kinds where user interacts with it - what is a potentially better UX and work our way backwards from that. Add the example of thinking machine labs release blog and video.
- We'll find that AI driven data fetch and UIs are the most flexible and cna work with structured or unstructured data and multi modal.
- How do agents work? Explain tool calling and how it's perhaps a one time effort developing the harness with little maintenance beyond fine tuning the model, observation and security. Though we're already seeing models like Mythos that can solve the security issue.
- So much of what swe is today, most of the jobs, will likely be obsolete in this model and the other core software like OS, database etc the foundational blocks are already mature and there aren't that many jobs there. Same goes for games though VR or even AI itself might create new genres and increase activity here.
- So AI doesn't have to replace swe as it is today dealing with large codebases and context because the nature of most apps will change. This is the core thesis of hard problems solved by replacement. A bit cliche but can give example of cars replacing horses even when the obvious problem was that horses shat a lot and were slower than what people wanted.
- But this gives rise to newer opportunities within tech and beyond: fine tuning the model, aligment, feeding it good data (gathering and cleaning), analysing the responses is what's left on the technical side. Basically just understanding and improving the models and how we collaborate with them. These are new jobs and with data privacy and security becoming very important again, every company would likely have a team of people doing this internally.
